Rose AI search is the fastest way to find candidates. Rather than setting filters by hand, you describe who you're looking for in plain language — the same way you'd brief a recruiter — and Rose does the research.

## The Rose home screen

Click **Rose** in the sidebar (or **New chat**). You'll see **“Hi, how can I help?”** and a message box. A few things live around it:

<Image src="https://blob-cdn.documentation.ai/org-6838a7a8-9bb8-4a3f-bda5-450ea34dc262/doc-c7e6ca58-4630-4814-b064-42b77b2b78ad/1791067537534-vg4736fhe1-rose-home.png?fm=auto&auto=compress%2Cformat" alt="The Rose home screen with the greeting, company context and message box" />


- **Working with** — your company context from the [Knowledge Base](/getting-started/knowledge-base), with an **Edit** link.
- **Project** — choose **Start a new project** or pick one of **Your projects**, so the candidates you find land in the right place from the first message.
- **Mode** — **Planning** or **Normal**. See [Planning Mode](/how-rose-works/planning-mode).
- **Paperclip** — **Upload a job description** (PDF, DOCX or TXT) and Rose reads it for you.
- **Exclude** — keep people you've already sourced out of the results.
- **Browse skills** — see [what Rose can do](/how-rose-works/skills), with example prompts.

## Briefing Rose

Type a description of the role and the person. Be as specific or as broad as you like:

> *“I'm looking for an Account Executive with 3–5 years of experience at a New York tech startup”*
>
> *“Find me a senior DevOps engineer in London or Manchester with AWS and Kubernetes experience”*

You can name several cities in one brief. Rose treats **Remote** as a way of working, not a place, so she won't search for people who happen to live “in Remote”.

### Rose asks what she needs

Before she searches, Rose makes sure she has the essentials. When something is missing she asks — often several questions at once on a single card, with suggestions you can tap and room to type your own answer. Answer in any order; Rose keeps track of what she still needs.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Before her first search, Rose asks for the job description. Paste it, upload it — or, if you haven't written one yet, choose **Draft with me** and she'll build it with you from a few answers.
</Callout>

## How Rose searches

Rose picks the right approach for the brief:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Calibrated search" icon="target">
    For most roles, Rose shows you **three profiles at a time** and learns from your verdicts until the search is right. This is the most precise way to search. See [Calibrating a Search](/finding-candidates/calibration).
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Real-time search" icon="zap">
    When you already know exactly who you want — named companies, a specific title — Rose searches live and groups the results into named clusters you can review and add from.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Company search" icon="building-2">
    Describe the kind of company you want to hire from and Rose finds them first — competitors, adjacent markets — then sources from the ones you pick.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Rose can also search the web while she works — to look up a company, a product or a market — and shows you what she found.

<Callout kind="tip">
  **The experience number is smarter than it looks.** Rose recomputes each candidate's *relevant* years of experience — counting only post-graduation, in-function work and excluding internships, student jobs and unrelated roles.
</Callout>

## Adding candidates to a project

Rose never adds anyone to a project without your say-so. Candidates join a project when you mark them a good match during calibration (with **Add liked candidates to project** switched on), or when you ask Rose to add them. Once they're in, you'll find them on the project's pipeline — see [Managing a Project](/projects/managing-a-project).

## Refining in conversation

Because the search lives in a chat, you can steer Rose like a colleague — narrow by location, seniority or companies; cap how many candidates come from one employer; set a minimum experience bar; or tell her what you liked and didn't. Every chat is saved under **Recent** in the sidebar, so you can come back to it later.

<Callout kind="warning">
  Rose is AI and can make mistakes. Always review profiles before you launch outreach.
</Callout>
