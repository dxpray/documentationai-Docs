---
title: Rose AI Search Tips
description: Brief Rose clearly, give reasons when you calibrate, and steer her in conversation to get sharper shortlists faster.
---

**Be specific, but conversational.** Rose responds well to natural language that describes the role clearly. Include seniority, location, industry, and any non-negotiables upfront — or upload the job description.

> Good: *“I need a founding AE with 4+ years B2B SaaS experience, based in London or remote UK”*
>
> Less effective: *“sales person”*

**Say why when you calibrate.** When Rose shows you three candidates, a reason like “too junior”, “wrong industry” or “exactly this background” teaches her far more than a verdict on its own. See [Calibrating a Search](/finding-candidates/calibration).

**Name companies when you know them.** If you already know where your candidates work today, say so — *“Search Stripe, Adyen and Checkout.com for senior backend engineers”* — and Rose searches in real time. Not sure which companies? Ask her to find companies like the ones you admire first.

**Keep the conversation going.** If results aren't quite right, tell Rose what to change — location, seniority, companies to include or avoid — and she'll adjust the search rather than starting over.

**Skip familiar faces.** Starting a new chat for a role you've sourced before? Use **Exclude** in the message box to leave out anyone already in a project.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Fill out your [Knowledge Base](/getting-started/knowledge-base) once and Rose carries your company context into every chat — so you don't have to repeat it.
</Callout>
